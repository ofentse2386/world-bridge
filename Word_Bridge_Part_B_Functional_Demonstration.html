Welcome

Build a bridge from the language you know to English

Real-world task: Explore a community scene, discuss its meaning, collect vocabulary, create a bilingual caption, analyse visual choices, and share your story with a peer.

Learning objectives: By the end of the activity, learners should be able to use relevant English vocabulary and structures, communicate ideas through speaking and writing, interpret colour/size/position in an image, collaborate with peers, and explain how a home language supported English learning.

Teacher Guide · Part B

How to use Word Bridge in a Grade 6 EFAL classroom

Purpose: Word Bridge responds to the linguistic wall created when English is treated as the only accepted language for thinking, speaking and writing. Learners use their home languages as resources for meaning-making and then develop English output.

Target learners

Grade 6 EFAL learners in a multilingual South African classroom.

Learning goals

Learners develop English vocabulary and structures, communicate through speaking and writing, interpret visual features, collaborate and reflect on their language resources.

Teacher role

Facilitate, model, question, support and assess. The teacher is not the only source of language knowledge.

Learner agency

Learners choose community content, contribute language knowledge and select how they present their story.

The learning journey

1 · Look

Click hotspots and connect English vocabulary to a familiar home-language word.

2 · Talk

Discuss the picture in any language, then agree on useful English words.

3 · Make

Create a home-language caption first, then an English caption, with optional audio/photo.

4 · Read the image

Interpret colour, size and position and explain how visual choices communicate meaning.

5 · Share

Exchange stories, give one kind comment and one question, then reflect.

EFAL skills covered

Listening & SpeakingReading & ViewingWriting & PresentingVocabulary & Structures

The five stages deliberately connect language input, interaction and output to these EFAL areas.

The thinking behind Word Bridge

Translanguaging

Learners use their full linguistic resources to understand and show what they know.

Garcia, Johnson & Seltzer (2017); Wei (2018)

Interdependence & identity

Home-language knowledge can support English learning, while identity is recognised as part of learning.

Cummins (2021)

Visual literacy

Learners interpret visual choices such as colour, size and position as part of meaning.

Kress & van Leeuwen (2021)

Multimedia learning

Words, pictures and sound are combined to support understanding.

Mayer (2021)

Learner roles

Photographer / content collectorLanguage keeperWriter / editorPresenter

Learners can work in pairs or groups. Stronger English speakers are not positioned as the teachers; each learner contributes expertise from their own linguistic and community knowledge.

South African classroom context and inclusion

  • Learners' communities are used as meaningful content.
  • Home languages are treated as learning resources rather than barriers.
  • Parents and elders can contribute community words or explanations where appropriate.
  • The artefact can be used on phones, tablets or computers, including shared devices.
  • The single HTML file can be saved and opened locally, reducing dependence on continuous internet access.
  • Audio recording is optional; learners can use the text submission when microphone access is unavailable.

Teacher demonstration checklist

  1. Open Start and explain the linguistic wall and purpose.
  2. Open Look and click all three hotspots.
  3. Use Talk to show home-language vocabulary leading to English vocabulary.
  4. Use Make to enter a bilingual caption and demonstrate audio/text submission.
  5. Use Analyse to demonstrate visual literacy.
  6. Use Read to show immediate quiz feedback.
  7. Use Share to demonstrate peer feedback and reflection.
  8. Open Assessment to show how evidence of learning is collected.
Live showcase tip: Invite the audience to complete only the Look, Make and Read activities on their phones if time is limited. The complete learner journey remains available in the artefact.
Step 1 · Look

Explore the community picture

Click each hotspot. Hear the English word and connect it to a word you already know. The aim is not to replace the home language with English, but to use familiar language as a bridge to English.

Start by clicking hotspot 1, 2 or 3.
Step 2 · Talk

Talk about what you see

Use any language to build meaning. Then agree on English words with your partner.

Prompt A

What is happening in this community?

Prompt B

Which people, places, objects or actions can you name?

Prompt C

How is this similar to or different from your community?

Step 3 · Make

Create a bilingual multimodal story

Meaning first, English second. You can use the sample community image or upload a photo from your own community if your teacher permits it.

For the live demonstration, record a short English sentence. If browser recording is unavailable, type the sentence below as the simulated voice submission.

No recording yet.
Step 4 · Analyse

Analyse the meaning

Look closely at the visual choices. Explain how the image communicates meaning.

Colour

Choose one colour you notice and explain what it helps you notice.

Position

What is placed on the left, right, centre, top or bottom? Why might that matter?

Size

Which element attracts attention because of its size?

“The shop is on the left and the people are near it. This makes the shop an important part of the community scene. The people show that the place is used by the community.”
Step 5 · Read & respond

Check your understanding

1. Where is the shop positioned?

2. Which combination can create meaning in a digital story?

3. Why do we begin with a home language?

Choose an answer.
Step 6 · Share

Peer feedback and reflection

Exchange stories. Your partner must give one kind comment and one useful question.
